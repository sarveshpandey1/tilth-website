How much should you spend on Google Ads in India to start?

"What's the minimum budget?" is the question every founder asks, and it's the wrong first one. Google publishes no minimum. Here's how to work a starting budget out of your own margin, your own cost-per-click and the number of conversions you need before the result means anything.

Anuja Founder, Tilth · 10+ years in performance marketing

"How much should I spend on Google Ads?" is usually the first question a founder asks before running paid ads in India. The uncomfortable answer is that nobody can give you a number without your numbers — and that what you're really looking for isn't a minimum spend, it's the point at which spend starts producing a decision. This guide walks through how to work a starting Google Ads budget for a startup in India out of your own margin and your own cost-per-click.

Google doesn't publish a minimum

Worth saying plainly, because a lot of advice implies otherwise: Google Ads has no stated minimum campaign budget. What you set is an average daily budget, and Google's own documentation is specific about how it's spent. Daily spend varies — Google leans into days where clicks and conversions are likelier — but the variance is capped: for most campaigns you won't pay more than twice your average daily budget on any single day, or more than 30.4 times it in a month. The 30.4 isn't arbitrary; it's 365 divided by 12. So your monthly exposure is knowable before you launch.

Work out the ceiling before the floor

The first number isn't what you can spend. It's the most you can afford to pay for one customer, and it comes out of gross margin, not revenue. A ₹4,000 order at 25% margin leaves ₹1,000 of contribution; pay ₹1,200 to acquire it and you've bought a loss at scale. For a considered purchase, run the arithmetic one step further back — average deal value, times margin, times the rate at which leads become customers. Paying lead-level prices against customer-level expectations is the most common way a budget looks fine for two months and then doesn't.

Then the two numbers that set the floor

Expected cost-per-click

Keyword Planner is the place to look, though Google requires billing information to be entered before basic features like keyword ideas are available. Don't hunt for a single "CPC" figure; that isn't what it gives you. The relevant columns are Top of page bid (low range) and (high range) — Google describes them as approximations of the 20th and 80th percentile of bids over the last 30 days — and the forecast view separately estimates Clicks, Cost, Impressions, CTR and Avg. CPC for a given bid. Treat all of it as an order of magnitude: Google says forecasts rest on roughly a week of data, that new advertisers get all-advertiser averages, and that accuracy drops for small geographies, which describes most first campaigns in India rather precisely.

Expected conversion rate

This is where budget plans quietly fail, because the rate gets assumed rather than estimated. If you have organic or referral traffic to the same page, use that rate and discount it — paid traffic from a broad query is rarely as qualified as someone who came looking. If you have no data, say so in the plan and treat the first weeks as the exercise that produces the number.

How much evidence before the result means anything

Google publishes a figure here, and it's worth quoting precisely. To evaluate Smart Bidding results accurately, Google Ads recommends measuring performance over longer periods containing at least 30 conversions — a month or more — and at least 50 for Target ROAS. (Google also notes that from June 2026 the strategies formerly called "Maximize conversions with a Target CPA" and "Maximize conversion value with a Target ROAS" are simply Target CPA and Target ROAS.)

That is a platform benchmark for judging a bidding strategy — not a statistical rule, and not automatically your decision threshold. Google doesn't present it as a significance test, and it notes that data recommendations vary by campaign type. It's also a different question from eligibility: Target CPA can be switched on with no conversion history at all, while Target ROAS carries its own per-campaign-type requirements, which for Search and Shopping are at least 15 conversions in the past 30 days. Being allowed to use a strategy and having enough data to judge it are not the same test.

Your own threshold depends on what you're testing and what one conversion is worth. If you sell a large contract, a handful of enquiries with qualification and pipeline data attached can be more informative than thirty form fills, because the evidence lives downstream of the ad account. A high-volume ecommerce campaign may pass thirty in a week and still want considerably more before changing anything — especially when the real question is creative or landing page rather than bidding. Decide in advance what would change your mind and what you'd need to see to believe it, then check whether the budget can buy that.

A budget too small to generate a decision doesn't fail slowly. It just never tells you anything, and you pay for the silence.

Illustrative arithmetic — not a benchmark

The numbers below are invented to show the shape of the calculation. Don't borrow them; they're arithmetic, not a market rate. The 30-conversion figure is borrowed from Google's Smart Bidding evaluation benchmark purely as an illustrative volume — substitute whatever evidence your own decision actually needs.

InputIllustrative figure
Most you can pay per customer (from margin)₹1,000
Assumed cost-per-click₹30
Assumed landing-page conversion rate2%
Implied cost per conversion (₹30 ÷ 2%)₹1,500
Illustrative evaluation volume30 conversions
Implied spend at that volume (30 × ₹1,500)₹45,000

Illustrative arithmetic only. Substitute your own margin, bid range and conversion rate — the output changes completely, which is the point.

Two things fall out of that immediately. The implied cost per conversion (₹1,500) is above the ceiling the margin allows (₹1,000), so the campaign isn't viable as specified — and you know that before spending, which is the whole value of the exercise. And ₹45,000 isn't the price of certainty; it's simply the spend these illustrative assumptions imply to reach thirty conversions. Spreading it over one month or two changes how fast that evidence arrives, not how much of it you get.

What else moves your number

  • Search demand. Google can only capture demand that exists. Check query volume before you check your bank balance.
  • Geographic scope. One city is cheaper to saturate and faster to read than all of India.
  • Sales-cycle length. If the cycle is 60 days, a 30-day test measures leads, not customers.
  • Campaign type. Search is easiest to diagnose, because you can see which queries spent the money. Shopping needs a clean feed. Performance Max draws on all Google inventory from one campaign — strong once you have a baseline, awkward while you're still working out what's happening.
  • AI Max. Worth knowing what it isn't: Google describes it as an optimisation layer activated inside existing Search campaigns, not a campaign type. Switching it on enables search term matching plus text customisation and Final URL expansion, each toggleable. On a diagnostic first test, know which are on — they change what your spend reaches.
  • Runway. Three months you can sustain beats one month you can't. A campaign switched off mid-learning gives the worst return there is: full cost, no conclusion.

How to tell the budget is too small

One division settles it: monthly budget ÷ expected cost per conversion. If the answer is in low single digits, the campaign can't produce a decision inside a month and no amount of skill changes that. Three honest options then — narrow the targeting until cost per conversion drops (tighter keywords, one city, one product, your highest-intent queries), extend the runway so conversions accumulate, or don't start yet. Structuring tightly enough to learn on a small budget is a real discipline, and it's most of what good paid media management does in month one.

What eats the budget, and when to increase it

Founders usually assume a disappointing result means the budget was too small. In the accounts we audit, the more common cause is that conversion tracking wasn't reporting correctly — so automated bidding was optimising toward an incomplete signal, or none. Adding budget doesn't fix that; it spends the mistake faster. Run a quick ad spend self-audit first; getting the signal right before spend scales is the core of growth strategy and measurement, and it's cheaper than any month of ads.

Then increase only when you have a cost per acquisition you trust, measured over a window with enough conversions to be real, sitting inside the ceiling your margin allows. Scaling earlier doesn't accelerate learning — it multiplies whatever is or isn't working, at a higher price. Same logic as an overall startup marketing budget: the measurement has to be right before the number gets bigger.

Google Ads budget for India startups — FAQs

Google doesn't publish one. You set an average daily budget, and Google's documentation states you'll never pay more than twice that on a single day, or 30.4 times it in a month, for most campaigns. The real floor is a question about your numbers: can this budget buy enough conversions to tell you anything?

Start with the most you can afford to pay for a customer — from gross margin, not revenue. Estimate cost-per-click from Keyword Planner's top-of-page bid ranges, assume a conversion rate you can defend, multiply the conversions you need by your expected cost per conversion, and spread that over the weeks you can afford to run.

Google recommends evaluating Smart Bidding over periods containing at least 30 conversions, or 50 for Target ROAS. That's a platform benchmark for judging a bidding strategy, not a statistical rule — and it's separate from eligibility, which differs by campaign type. Your own threshold depends on what you're testing and what a conversion is worth: a high-value B2B campaign can learn from fewer conversions when lead quality and pipeline data are available, while a high-volume ecommerce test may want considerably more.

A tightly scoped Search campaign is usually easiest to diagnose, because you can see which queries spent the money. Shopping suits a clean product feed. Performance Max is powerful but harder to attribute. AI Max isn't a campaign type — Google describes it as an optimisation layer inside a Search campaign.

Divide the monthly budget by your expected cost per conversion. If the answer is in low single digits, no amount of skill produces a decision inside a month. Narrow the targeting until cost per conversion falls, extend the runway, or wait.
