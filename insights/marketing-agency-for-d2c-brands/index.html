How to choose a marketing agency for your D2C brand in India

D2C marketing fails differently from B2B — and most agencies pitch the same generic deck either way. Here's what actually matters when choosing a marketing agency for a D2C brand in India: what to look for, the questions to ask, and the red flags that should make you walk.

Anuja Founder, Tilth · 10+ years in performance marketing

For a direct-to-consumer brand, marketing isn't a support function — it is the business. You live and die by acquisition cost, repeat purchase rate and contribution margin, so the wrong marketing agency for your D2C brand doesn't just waste a retainer; it burns the ad budget that is your growth. Here's the buyer's guide I'd give a founder choosing one in India.

1. Real D2C experience — with brands like yours

D2C has its own physics: thin margins, returns, repeat-purchase economics, marketplace versus own-site dynamics. An agency that's only run B2B lead-gen will apply the wrong playbook. Ask for work with D2C brands at a similar category and stage — and ask what didn't work, not just the highlight reel.

2. They talk in contribution margin, not ROAS

ROAS is revenue divided by ad spend and says nothing about whether you made money: a 3x on 25% contribution margin loses cash, a 2x on 60% doesn't. Agencies worth hiring ask for landed cost, shipping, payment fees, returns and discount load in the first conversation, and talk in break-even ROAS or blended MER. If nobody asks what a sale is actually worth to you, they're optimising a number that can rise while your bank balance falls.

3. New and repeat customers are separated — always

The most useful discipline in D2C reporting, and the one most often missing. Blended ROAS flatters you when repeat customers were buying anyway: paid takes credit for revenue it didn't cause and you scale into a wall. Ask to see new-customer CAC and new-customer contribution separately from total. An agency that can't split them can't tell you whether acquisition works — and retention is where D2C margin lives, so the second and tenth order need an owner too.

4. Tracking they'll fix, and reconcile against your backend

Every scaling decision rides on whether reported ROAS and CAC are real. Browser-only tracking misses conversions — how many varies, and anyone quoting a precise loss figure is guessing — and platform totals routinely disagree with Shopify. Some disagreement is normal, since the platforms count differently and claim overlapping credit. What matters is whether somebody reconciles platform orders against backend orders regularly and can explain the gap. Fix conversion tracking before handing over a budget; rebuilding it properly is measurement work, not media work.

For a D2C brand, the agency's media skill matters less than whether the numbers it's optimising toward are true.

5. Creative volume, with a hypothesis behind it

On Meta especially, creative is the main lever and fatigue is the normal state. Ask two things: how many new concepts a month, and what happens to the losers. Volume without a hypothesis is churn — you want to hear what was being tested, what it showed, and what came next. Then ask how long brief-to-live takes: a two-week turnaround quietly caps how fast you can learn, whatever the media plan says.

6. They can change the page, not just the ad

Sending more traffic to a store that doesn't convert is the most expensive way to "scale". Ask who may change the product and checkout pages, and whether CRO is in scope or an excluded extra. Same for merchandising and promotions — bundles, thresholds and discount depth move contribution margin faster than most bid changes, and an agency with no view of them is working one-handed.

7. Channel mix chosen for your economics

D2C in India spans Meta and Google, but also marketplaces, quick commerce and influencer — and those carry different economics. Marketplace revenue arrives with commission, fulfilment terms and no customer data; owned-store revenue costs more to acquire and gives you the relationship. A serious agency says which mix suits your margin instead of blending both into one ROAS. It should also coordinate Meta and Google rather than running them as rival scoreboards — branded search will happily claim demand that paid social created. Doing that deliberately is the substance of performance marketing for D2C and ecommerce brands.

Red flags worth walking away from

  • Reporting stops at platform ROAS — no contribution margin, no blended view, no backend reconciliation.
  • They can't separate new from repeat customers — so they can't tell you if acquisition works.
  • Accounts and tracking in the agency's name, or raw data available only through their dashboard.
  • Creative with no hypothesis, or a turnaround too slow for the rate the channel burns assets.
  • Guaranteed ROAS, or one generic deck that could belong to any client.

The shortcut: know your own numbers first

Get an independent read on your own setup before you start interviewing. Once you know whether tracking is sound, what a new customer really costs and which channels clear your margin, you can judge every pitch against reality rather than on faith. That's what a foundation audit gives you.

Choosing a D2C marketing agency — FAQs

Look for genuine D2C experience with similar brands, a full-funnel approach that values retention (not just acquisition), honest tracking and attribution, reporting tied to revenue and margin, and clear answers on who runs your account.
